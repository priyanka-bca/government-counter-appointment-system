Government Counter Appointment Booking System
System Analysis Work Sample

Prepared by: Piyanka Ghimire  
 
Project status: Conceptual case study — not implemented

1. Overview

This work sample explores how a proposed appointment-booking system could help citizens schedule visits to a government service counter.

The case study focuses on understanding a potential service-delivery problem, defining system requirements, mapping workflows, identifying acceptance criteria, and considering risks and unresolved operational decisions.

It is intended to demonstrate my approach to system analysis and requirements documentation.

2. Problem Context

Repeated visits, uncertainty about service availability, and manual appointment coordination can create difficulties for citizens and counter staff.

This case study considers a proposed digital approach to appointment scheduling. The problem context is illustrative and has not been validated through stakeholder interviews, field research, or observation of an actual government office.

3. Contents

The work sample covers:

- Problem statement, objectives, scope, assumptions, and constraints
- Stakeholder identification and proposed workflow
- Functional and non-functional requirements
- Appointment status transitions
- Acceptance criteria and requirements traceability
- Risks, assumptions, and open questions
- Proposed validation and next steps

4. Key Analysis Areas

Requirements analysis: Defines the expected system behaviour and distinguishes confirmed requirements from decisions that still require stakeholder input.

Workflow design: Illustrates the proposed appointment lifecycle, including booking, confirmation, cancellation, rescheduling, check-in, and completion.

Acceptance criteria: Connects functional requirements to observable conditions that could be used during future testing.

Risk analysis: Considers issues such as slot overbooking, failed notifications, service closures, access barriers, and operational disruption.

Requirements traceability: Links requirements to proposed acceptance criteria and supporting workflow illustrations.

5. Important Limitations

This is an independent, hypothetical case study prepared for demonstration purposes. It is not an SDOC assignment or an approved government project.

No stakeholder interviews, field research, software implementation, or user testing have been conducted. Requirements, priorities, and performance targets are proposals that would need validation with the relevant stakeholders.

The document does not claim to represent the actual processes or requirements of any specific government office.
